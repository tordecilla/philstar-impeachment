Introduction

This is Working Draft's audio overview of the July 6, 2026 impeachment proceedings against Vice President Sara Duterte. This is a working reference, not an official Senate record.

02

The big picture

July 6 was an opening and organizational session. After debating who should preside, the impeachment court elected Francis “Chiz” Escudero, heard the parties' opening statements, set initial procedural directions, and adjourned without witness testimony or admitted documentary evidence.

What happened

With 21 senators present, the court declared a quorum. Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian promised impartial justice under the Constitution and a fair opportunity for both sides to present their cases.

What happened

A long threshold debate followed over who should preside. Alan Peter Cayetano argued that the Constitution required the Senate President to take the chair and questioned the amended rules. Other senator-judges offered competing constitutional and procedural interpretations.

The court's directions

Escudero ruled that conviction would require at least 16 affirmative votes, while leaving the parties free to seek judicial relief. He said the prosecution carries the burden and the respondent is presumed innocent.

The prosecution's position

Lead prosecutor Gerville Luistro framed the case as a question of public accountability involving public money, public trust, and citizens' right to demand accountability from their leaders. She presented the four articles as chapters of one alleged pattern of power exercised without accountability.

The prosecution's position

On Article 1, the prosecution alleged that more than 612 million pesos in confidential funds of the Office of the Vice President and the Department of Education were disbursed, transferred, and liquidated under circumstances it said could not withstand scrutiny. It cited questionable liquidation records and receipts bearing names it said government records could not verify. These were allegations, not findings made that day.
